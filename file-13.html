File #13 · Long

They Saw a Fish 7 Miles Down. Science Says That's Impossible.

ALL HANDS

The dossier

Key facts

Date
January 23, 1960 (Project Nekton)
Location
Challenger Deep, Mariana Trench, southwest of Guam
Vessel
Bathyscaphe Trieste
Pilots
Jacques Piccard and U.S. Navy Lt. Don Walsh
Depth
About 35,797 ft on the 1960 corrected gauge. Modern measurement: 10,935 m ± 6 m
Pressure
More than 16,000 psi
The sighting
Piccard reported a flatfish about 1 foot long. There is no photograph. Later science says a bony fish almost certainly cannot live this deep.

Where it happened

Challenger Deep, Mariana Trench, southwest of Guam (approximate; exact dive track not shown). View larger map · Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Timeline

  1. Jan 23, 1960Descent takes 4 hours 47 minutes. A bang / shudder at about 32,400 feet.
  2. About 20 minutes on the bottomPiccard, at the window, says he sees a fish.
  3. Ascent, 3 hours 15 minutesThe viewing port in the already-flooded entrance tube has cracked; the sphere itself holds.
  4. Mar 26, 2012James Cameron makes a solo dive in Deepsea Challenger. Small amphipods; no fish.
  5. Apr 28, 2019Victor Vescovo's first Limiting Factor dive to Challenger Deep. Amphipods, sea cucumbers, tracks, and man-made debris.
  6. After 1960In 1963 Trieste helps locate debris from USS Thresher. She is now on exhibit at the National Museum of the U.S. Navy, Washington Navy Yard.
