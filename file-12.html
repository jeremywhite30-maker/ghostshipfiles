File #12 · Long

One Destroyer Charged a Japanese Battleship Fleet. USS Johnston's Last Stand

ALL HANDS

The dossier

Key facts

Date
October 25, 1944, at the Battle off Samar
Location
Philippine Sea, off Samar
Vessel
USS Johnston (DD-557), Fletcher-class destroyer
Commander
Cmdr. Ernest E. Evans, posthumous Medal of Honor
Crew
327 aboard. 186 died (some counts say 187); 141 rescued after about 50 hours at sea.
Cause
Sunk by gunfire from the Japanese force; she rolled over and sank at 10:10 a.m.
Status
Wreck identified March 31, 2021, at about 21,180 ft. It was the deepest wreck ever surveyed until Samuel B. Roberts in 2022.

Where it happened

General area of the Battle off Samar (approximate; exact wreck position not shown). View larger map · Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Timeline

  1. Oct 25, 1944, morningJohnston charges a Japanese force of battleships, cruisers and destroyers to shield the escort carriers.
  2. 10:10 a.m.Johnston rolls over and sinks. Commander Evans is seen abandoning ship but is never seen again.
  3. ~50 hours later141 survivors are rescued.
  4. Oct 30, 2019RV Petrel finds wreckage believed to be Johnston.
  5. Mar 31, 2021Victor Vescovo's DSV Limiting Factor dives, surveys and confirms Johnston at about 21,180 ft.
