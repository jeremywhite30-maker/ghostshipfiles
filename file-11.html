File #11 · Long

The Edmund Fitzgerald Radioed "We Are Holding Our Own." Then She Vanished

ALL HANDS

The dossier

Key facts

Date
November 10, 1975, shortly after 7:10 p.m.
Location
Lake Superior, Canadian (Ontario) waters, about 17 miles from Whitefish Bay
Vessel
SS Edmund Fitzgerald, 729-ft lake freighter (launched June 7, 1958)
Crew
All 29 men lost
Cause
Disputed. The NTSB and Coast Guard blamed flooding through the hatches; others point to shoaling or giant waves.
Status
Lies in two pieces in 530 ft of water. Her bell was recovered July 4, 1995.

Where it happened

Wreck site, eastern Lake Superior (46.9985°N 85.1102°W). View larger map · Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Timeline

  1. Nov 9, 1975Leaves Superior, Wisconsin, with taconite pellets for a steel mill near Detroit. Captain Ernest M. McSorley is in command.
  2. Nov 10, afternoonCaught in a severe storm with hurricane-force winds. Her radar fails, and the Arthur M. Anderson helps guide her.
  3. Nov 10, 7:10 p.m.Last message to the Anderson: "We are holding our own."
  4. Minutes laterShe sinks suddenly. There is no mayday.
  5. Nov 14, 1975A U.S. Navy aircraft detecting magnetic anomalies locates the wreck.
  6. May 20–28, 1976The Navy's CURV-III submersible finds her in two large pieces in 530 ft of water.
  7. Jul 4, 1995The ship's bell is recovered. It's now at the Great Lakes Shipwreck Museum.
