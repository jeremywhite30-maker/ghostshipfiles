Found adrift, crew gone

Documented ghost ships

  1. Mary Celeste Documented

    1872 · Atlantic, off the Azores

    On December 4, 1872, the brigantine Dei Gratia found the American brigantine Mary Celeste drifting under partial sail, seaworthy, with her cargo of alcohol intact and her lifeboat missing. The last log entry was ten days old. Captain Benjamin Briggs, his wife, their young daughter and seven crew were never seen again. A salvage hearing in Gibraltar weighed foul play but found no convincing evidence.

    Sources: Gibraltar salvage hearing record (1873); Paul Begg, Mary Celeste: The Greatest Mystery of the Sea; Brian Hicks, Ghost Ship; Wikipedia

  2. Carroll A. Deering Documented Ghost Ship File

    1921 · Diamond Shoals, North Carolina

    The five-masted schooner was found aground off Cape Hatteras on January 31, 1921, with her sails set and her lifeboats gone. When she was boarded on February 4, nobody was aboard, and the crew was never found. Several federal departments investigated, and no one closed the case. Open the File →

    Sources: Bland Simpson, Ghost Ship of Diamond Shoals; David Stick, Graveyard of the Atlantic; Wikipedia

  3. SS Baychimo Documented

    1931–1969 · Arctic coast of Alaska

    The Hudson's Bay Company cargo steamer was trapped in pack ice near Barrow (now Utqiagvik), Alaska, in October 1931. After a November blizzard she vanished from her mooring in the ice, and her crew assumed she had sunk. She hadn't. For decades people reported seeing the abandoned ship drifting along the Alaskan coast, the last time in 1969. What finally happened to her is unknown.

    Sources: Anthony Dalton, Baychimo: Arctic Ghost Ship; Wikipedia

  4. MV Joyita Documented

    1955 · South Pacific

    Joyita left Apia, Samoa, on October 3, 1955, bound for the Tokelau Islands with 25 passengers and crew. On November 10 she was found drifting north of Vanua Levu, Fiji, more than 600 miles off her route, partly flooded and with nobody aboard. A 1956 inquiry called the fate of those aboard inexplicable on the evidence.

    Sources: 1956 Commission of Inquiry (Apia); Robin Maugham, The Joyita Mystery; Wikipedia

  5. High Aim 6 Documented

    2003 · off Western Australia

    The Taiwanese longline fishing boat left Liuchiu, Taiwan, on October 31, 2002, and was found on January 8, 2003, drifting about 80 nautical miles east of Rowley Shoals with fuel, food and personal belongings aboard and no crew. Taiwanese investigators later suspected a mutiny in which the captain and engineer were killed.

    Sources: Central News Agency (Taiwan) reporting; Wikipedia

  6. Jian Seng Documented

    2006 · Gulf of Carpentaria, Australia

    An 80-metre tanker with no crew, no papers and her identifying marks painted over was spotted drifting about 180 km southwest of Weipa, Queensland, in March 2006, trailing a broken tow rope. Authorities never determined where she came from and scuttled her in deep water.

    Sources: Australian Customs statements, March 2006; Wikipedia

  7. Kaz II Documented

    2007 · Great Barrier Reef, Queensland

    The 9.8-metre catamaran left Airlie Beach on April 15, 2007. On April 18 a helicopter spotted her drifting, and when she was boarded on April 20 her engine was running, a laptop was on and food was set out. Her three-man crew was gone. A 2008 coroner's inquest looked at how they may have gone overboard. They were never found.

    Sources: Queensland Coroner's inquest (2008); Australian Maritime Safety Authority; Wikipedia

  8. MV Lyubov Orlova Documented

    2013 · North Atlantic

    The retired Antarctic cruise ship sat at St. John's, Newfoundland, for two years before she was towed out for scrap in 2013. She broke loose and became a derelict drifting in the North Atlantic with no one aboard. She is believed to have sunk.

    Sources: Wikipedia

Vanished without a trace

Ships that disappeared

  1. USS Cyclops Documented

    1918 · Atlantic, after Barbados

    The Navy collier Cyclops, overloaded with manganese ore, disappeared sometime after March 4, 1918, after an unscheduled stop at Barbados. All 306 crew and passengers were lost. Germany denied any part in it, and the Naval History and Heritage Command says she "probably sank in an unexpected storm." No wreck has been found.

    Sources: Naval History and Heritage Command; Wikipedia

  2. SS Waratah Documented

    1909 · off South Africa

    On only her second voyage, the Blue Anchor liner Waratah disappeared on the night of 27–28 July 1909 between Durban and Cape Town, with 92 passengers and 119 crew aboard. No wreckage or bodies have ever been found.

    Sources: Board of Trade inquiry; Wikipedia

  3. Texan schooner San Antonio Documented Ghost Ship File

    1842 · Gulf of Mexico

    After the only mutiny in Texas Navy history, at New Orleans on February 11, 1842, the schooner San Antonio was sent to Yucatán about October 1, 1842. She never reached Campeche and was never heard from again. Open the File →

    Sources: TSHA Handbook of Texas; Jonathan W. Jordan, Lone Star Navy; Wikipedia

Legends of the sea

Legends (no reliable record)

  1. The Flying Dutchman Legend

    Legend · Cape of Good Hope

    The legend of a Dutch ship doomed never to make port and to sail forever likely grew out of the Dutch East India Company's 17th-century trade around the Cape. The oldest surviving written versions date to the late 18th century, and sailors kept reporting glowing phantom ships for the next two centuries. It's a legend, not a documented vessel.

    Sources: Late-18th-century travel accounts, including A Voyage to Botany Bay (1795), attributed to George Barrington; Wikipedia

  2. Octavius Legend

    Legend · said to be 1775, off Greenland

    As the story goes, the whaler Herald found Octavius drifting west of Greenland on October 11, 1775, her crew frozen below decks and the captain dead at his desk with a log last written in 1762. No contemporary record backs it up, and the same story was told in the 1800s about other ships.

    Sources: No contemporary record; the tale circulated in 19th-century newspapers. Wikipedia

  3. Lady Lovibond Legend

    Legend · Goodwin Sands, England

    The schooner was said to have been wrecked on the Goodwin Sands on February 13, 1748, when a jealous first mate steered her aground during the captain's wedding voyage, and to reappear every 50 years. No contemporary record of the ship or the wreck has been found, and there was no reported sighting in 1998.

    Sources: No contemporary record. Wikipedia

  4. SS Ourang Medan Legend

    Legend · 1940s, Dutch East Indies

    In the story, a freighter sends a distress call, rescuers find the whole crew dead with terrified faces, and then the ship catches fire and sinks. Versions surfaced in 1940, 1948 and 1952 with shifting locations, and researchers have never found the ship in any official register.

    Sources: Not found in any shipping register; see Fortean Times coverage. Wikipedia
