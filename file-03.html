What If Texas Became Its Own Country Tomorrow?

LOST AT SEA

The dossier

Key facts

Date
Sailed about October 1, 1842, and never arrived
Location
Gulf of Mexico, bound for Yucatán. Position never known.
Vessel
San Antonio, two-masted schooner of the Second Texas Navy (built in Baltimore as Asp)
Crew
All hands lost
Cause
Unknown
Status
Never found

Where it happened

POSITION UNKNOWNSan Antonio vanished somewhere in the Gulf of Mexico. No wreck site has ever been found.

Timeline

  1. Jun 26, 1840Sails from Galveston with Commodore Edwin W. Moore's flotilla.
  2. Feb 11, 1842Mutiny at New Orleans: Lieutenant Charles Fuller is killed. It's the only mutiny in Texas Navy history.
  3. Jul 1, 1842Rejoins the fleet at New Orleans.
  4. ~Oct 1, 1842Sent to Yucatán by way of Galveston and Matagorda to raise funds. She never reaches Campeche.
  5. Apr 26, 1843Four mutineers are hanged from the yardarm of the Texas sloop-of-war Austin.

Sources

  • TSHA Handbook of Texas
  • Jonathan W. Jordan, Lone Star Navy
  • Wikipedia: Texan schooner San Antonio

The story

Texas had its own navy. Then one of its ships vanished. In 1842 the schooner San Antonio sailed for Yucatán after a mutiny — and was never seen again.

What if Texas became its own country tomorrow? It already was once. Captain Eight opens the file on the Texas Navy: a mutiny in New Orleans, a ghost ship, and the day Texas called its own navy pirates. The modern scenes are a “what if.” The historical scenes follow the TSHA Handbook of Texas.
