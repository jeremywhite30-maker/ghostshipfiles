★ Lost Ship of the Month

SS Cornwallis, Gulf of Maine

A German U-boat torpedoed this 400-ft merchant ship on December 3, 1944, and only five of her 48 crew survived. The Nomad Exploration Team found her in nearly 400 ft of water after a seven-year search.

Found July 11, 2026 · reported Nov. 8, 2026 · Source: CBC News

  • Announced Oct. 14, 2026

    The world's oldest shipwreck, Kekova Island, Turkey

    Archaeologists date this trading-ship wreck to 4,400–4,700 years ago, in the Early Bronze Age. The wood is gone, but its stone anchors and more than 30 amphorae lie about 30 m down.

    Source: BBC News

  • Announced Aug. 10, 2026

    HMS Tiger, English Channel

    This torpedo boat destroyer was cut in two by the cruiser HMS Berwick during night exercises off the Isle of Wight in 1908, and fewer than half of her 57 crew survived. Divers from ProjectXplore found her after five years of archive research.

    Source: Royal Navy

  • Found June 28, 2025

    Schooner F.J. King, Lake Michigan

    Lost in a gale off Baileys Harbor, Wisconsin, on September 15, 1886, this wreck was a "ghost ship" to wreck hunters for 139 years. A citizen-science team found her nearly intact.

    Source: Wisconsin Underwater Archeology Association

  • Announced March 10, 2025

    Western Reserve, Lake Superior

    This steel freighter sank in a gale on August 30, 1892, and 27 of the 28 people aboard died. The Great Lakes Shipwreck Historical Society found her about 60 miles northwest of Whitefish Point, the same waters as the Edmund Fitzgerald.

    Source: Interlochen Public Radio
