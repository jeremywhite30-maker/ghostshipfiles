The Navy Didn't Know This Ship Sank. 900 Men Were in the Water

CASE CLOSED

The dossier

Key facts

Date
Torpedoed at 12:15 a.m., July 30, 1945
Location
Philippine Sea, between Guam and Leyte Gulf
Vessel
USS Indianapolis (CA-35), Portland-class heavy cruiser
Crew
1,195 aboard. About 300 went down with the ship; 316 survived.
Cause
Two torpedoes from Japanese submarine I-58; she sank in 12 minutes
Status
Wreck found August 19, 2017, about 18,000 ft down. Captain McVay exonerated by Congress in October 2000.

Where it happened

Approximate wreck location, Philippine Sea (12°02′N 134°48′E). View larger map · Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Timeline

  1. Jul 26, 1945Delivers atomic bomb components to Tinian after a secret high-speed run.
  2. Jul 30, 12:15 a.m.Hit by I-58. She sinks in 12 minutes, and about 890 men are left in the sea.
  3. Aug 2, 11:25 a.m.Lt. Wilbur "Chuck" Gwinn's PV-1 Ventura spots survivors on a routine patrol.
  4. Nov–Dec 1945Captain Charles B. McVay III is court-martialed and convicted of hazarding his ship by failing to zigzag.
  5. Oct 2000Congress resolves that McVay's record should state he is exonerated.
  6. Aug 19, 2017Paul Allen's search team finds the wreck about 18,000 ft down.

Nine hundred men in the water. The Navy didn't even know the ship was gone. Four days later, a broken antenna made one pilot look down.
