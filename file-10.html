133 Days on a Raft. Ships Saw Him and Kept Going

SOLE SURVIVOR

The dossier

Key facts

Date
Benlomond torpedoed November 23, 1942. Rescued April 5, 1943.
Location
Central Atlantic, near the equator, about 250 miles north of Brazil. Rescued off Pará, Brazil.
Vessel
SS Benlomond, British cargo ship (Ben Line)
Survivor
Poon Lim, Second Mess Steward, the only survivor
Cause
Two torpedoes from U-172; she sank in about two minutes
Record
133 days alone on an 8-foot wooden raft, still the Guinness record
Honor
British Empire Medal, awarded by King George VI

Where it happened

Approximate position where Benlomond was torpedoed (00°30′N 38°45′W). View larger map · Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Timeline

  1. Nov 23, 1942U-172 torpedoes Benlomond. Poon is washed overboard, and the ship sinks in about two minutes.
  2. Hours laterThe U-boat crew sees him in the water and waves him away. After about two hours he reaches a wooden raft.
  3. Weeks adriftHe fishes, catches seabirds and collects rainwater. U.S. Navy patrol planes see him once or twice, but a storm hides him.
  4. Apr 5, 1943Three Brazilian fishermen find him off Pará after he has drifted about 750 miles west.
  5. Three days laterHe is landed at Belém and can walk again.

The story

In 1942 a U-boat sank his ship. The crew saw him in the water and laughed. Then a cargo ship saw him on his raft and kept going.

Poon Lim, a steward who could barely swim, survived 133 days alone on an 8-foot wooden raft in the Atlantic. That's still the Guinness record for survival alone on a raft.

Captain Eight and Jeremy (Navy Veteran, GSM2) walk through how he did it: fishhooks from a flashlight spring, rainwater caught in life-jacket canvas, a shark fight, a Navy plane that lost him in a storm, and what the U.S. Navy put in his hand when he finally made it home.
