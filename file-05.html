The dossier

Key facts

Case
RMS Titanic, used to explain buoyancy and flooding
Date
Struck an iceberg at 11:40 p.m. on April 14, 1912, and sank at 2:20 a.m. on April 15
Location
North Atlantic, on her maiden voyage from Southampton to New York
Vessel
Olympic-class ocean liner
Lost
About 1,500 of roughly 2,200 aboard (estimates vary)
Cause
Iceberg collision opened the hull and flooded the forward compartments
Status
Wreck found September 1, 1985, broken in two

The story

A steel bolt sinks. A steel ship does not. Same metal. The difference is the hollow hull — and what happens when flooding steals the empty space.

Captain Eight follows the same physics all the way to Titanic: density, Archimedes’ principle, ballast and compensation water, and what changes when compartments flood.
